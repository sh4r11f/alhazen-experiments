# Mental rotation of 3-D objects

**Original.** Shepard, R. N., & Metzler, J. (1971). Mental rotation of three-dimensional
objects. *Science, 171*(3972), 701–703. doi:10.1126/science.171.3972.701. Experiment
replicated: the paper's single experiment (same/different judgements of 1600 pairs per
subject; picture-plane and depth rotations from 0 to 180 degrees). **Source read:** the
full text, from the JSTOR scan at
<https://www.cs.virginia.edu/~evans/cs1120-f09/ps/ps3/mental-rotation.pdf> (the paper is on
the PDF's pages 2–4, which are journal pages 701, 702 and 703; page numbers below are the
journal's).
**Status.** `original-protocol` (the design is the paper's; the apparatus substitutions are
in the deviations table, every unstated value in the assumed table). **Hardware.** A display
and a keyboard.

## Question

Can people tell that two pictures show the same three-dimensional object, and how long
does it take as a function of the angle between the portrayed orientations? The paper
found the time (i) a linearly increasing function of the angular difference and (ii) no
shorter for a rotation in the picture plane than for a rotation in depth (p. 701,
abstract), consistent with subjects imagining one object rotating into the other's
orientation at a limited rate, roughly 60 degrees per second (p. 703).

## Apparatus

*Original* (pp. 702–703). Perspective line drawings, generated by computer (18 projections
per object) and reproduced by photo-offset, attached in pairs to cards. The drawings
"averaged between 4 and 5 cm in maximum linear extent" and were viewed at about 60 cm,
"positioned, with a center-to-center spacing that subtended a visual angle of 9°, in two
circular apertures in a vertical black surface" (p. 703). The subject pulled a right-hand
lever for "same" and a left-hand lever for "different" (p. 702); a timer started with the
pair and was stopped by the lever, which also ended the display (p. 703). A warning tone
preceded each pair by half a second (p. 703).

*Here.* A display and a keyboard. The black surface fills the screen; two circular
apertures, [[param:spacing_dva]] = 9 deg apart, each show one image of the stimulus bank.
Sizes are in degrees of visual angle from the rig file's geometry (`configs/rig-booth.yaml`
is the example; enter your own measured panel and distance). Durations are whole display
frames at the measured refresh rate. Keys replace the levers (J right hand = same, F left
hand = different), and a visual warning dot replaces the tone (see Deviations).

## Stimuli

*Objects* (p. 702). "Each object consisted of ten solid cubes attached face-to-face to form
a rigid armlike structure with exactly three right-angled 'elbows'." Ten objects in two
subsets of five: "within either subset, no shape could be transformed into itself or any
other by any reflection or rotation (short of 360°). However, each shape in either subset
was the mirror image of one shape in the other subset." The objects were chosen to be
"relatively unfamiliar and meaningless in overall three-dimensional shape".

The original ten shapes are not reproduced. `src/shepard_metzler1971/bank.py` enumerates
every arm of 10 cubes with 3 right-angled elbows (each straight run at least 2 steps:
ASSUMED), keeps those with no rotational symmetry that are chiral (so no rotation or
reflection maps a shape onto itself), picks five with a fixed seed so that none is
congruent with or the mirror image of another (set A, objects 1–5), and adds their mirror
images (set B, objects 6–10; object k + 5 mirrors object k). The tests check every one of
the paper's constraints on the committed shapes.

*Views* (p. 702). "For each of the ten objects, 18 different perspective projections —
corresponding to one complete turn around the vertical axis by 20° steps — were generated
… Seven of the 18 perspective views of each object were then selected so as (i) to avoid
any views in which some part of the object was wholly occluded by another part and yet
(ii) to permit the construction of two pairs that differed in orientation by each possible
angle, in 20° steps, from 0° to 180°." Here the 18 views are perspective projections with
hidden lines removed, from a camera 25 deg above the horizontal and 25 cube edges away
(both ASSUMED), at azimuths 10, 30, …, 350 deg relative to the cube faces (ASSUMED). Rule
(i) is made numeric (every cube shows at least 20% of its unoccluded front-face area,
ASSUMED) and face-on views are excluded (ASSUMED); rule (ii) is the paper's. Among the
seven-view sets that pass, the one whose least visible cube is most visible is kept. That
gives 70 drawings, as in the paper.

*Drawings.* Black lines ([[param:line_width_dva]] = 0.04 deg, ASSUMED) on a white disc
that fills the aperture ([[param:aperture_dva]] = 6 deg, ASSUMED). All 70 are drawn at one
camera scale chosen so that their mean maximum linear extent is
[[param:drawing_extent_dva]] = 4.3 deg (4.5 cm at 60 cm, the midpoint of the paper's 4–5 cm:
ASSUMED); the individual drawings span 3.5–5.3 deg. Each is centred in its aperture.
The bank (`stimuli/bank/`, 70 PNGs, 0.4 MB, and `manifest.json`) is generated by
`uv run python -m shepard_metzler1971.bank` from `configs/task.yaml`; `--check` regenerates
it and compares byte for byte. The task refuses to run with a params file whose stimulus
values differ from the ones the bank was generated with.

## Procedure

One trial of [[task:mental-rotation]]:

1. **Inter-trial interval** ([[param:intertrial_interval]] = 1.5 s, ASSUMED): the black
   surface with two empty white apertures.
2. **Warning** ([[param:warning_duration]] = 0.5 s): "Each trial began with a warning tone,
   which was followed half a second later by the presentation of a stimulus pair and the
   simultaneous onset of a timer" (p. 703). Here a white dot ([[param:warning_dot_dva]] =
   0.3 deg, ASSUMED) between the apertures for the half second (a deviation: no tone).
3. **The pair**, until a key: "The lever-pulling response stopped the timer, recorded the
   subject's reaction time and terminated the visual display" (p. 703). J = same, F =
   different; RT is measured from the flip that showed the pair. A pair left unanswered
   for [[param:response_timeout]] = 30 s (ASSUMED) ends as NO_RESPONSE and is served again
   at the end of the block. Keys pressed before the pair is on screen are ignored.

Instructions (p. 703): "The subjects were instructed to respond as quickly as possible
while keeping errors to a minimum." The rest of the wording in `instructions.md` is ours.
No feedback and no practice trials (the paper describes neither; ASSUMED).

## Design

From the paper (p. 702): "each of eight adult subjects was presented with 1600 pairs". Of
these, 800 were "same" pairs, "400 unique pairs (20 'depth' and 20 'picture-plane' pairs at
each of the ten angular differences from 0° to 180°), each of which was presented twice",
and 800 "different" pairs, "400 unique 'different' pairs, each of which (again) was
presented twice. Each of these 'different' pairs corresponded to one 'same' pair (of
either the 'depth' or 'picture-plane' variety) in which, however, one of the
three-dimensional objects had been reflected about some plane in three-dimensional space."
A depth pair shows two views of one object that differ by a multiple of 20 deg about the
vertical axis; a picture-plane pair shows one view twice, the two copies differing in
orientation in the plane of the drawing (p. 702). "The 1600 pairs were grouped into blocks
of not more than 200 and presented over eight to ten 1-hour sessions"; each block "was
either 'pure', in that all pairs involved rotations of the same type … or 'mixed'"
(pp. 702–703).

Here (`src/shepard_metzler1971/design.py`; the counts are checked by `tests/test_design.py`):

- 800 unique pairs, the same for every subject: for each of the 10 objects, each rotation
  type and each of the 10 angles, 2 "same" pairs (ASSUMED split of the paper's 20 per type
  and angle), each with its "different" twin in which one drawing (random side) is
  replaced by the mirror object seen from the mirrored azimuth, i.e. the object reflected
  through the vertical plane of the line of sight. For "different" pairs the angle column
  holds the angle of the twin; as the paper notes (p. 703), no angle is defined for them.
- Each unique pair is presented twice: 1600 trials per subject, once in a pure and once in
  a mixed block (ASSUMED). Eight blocks of 200: 2 pure-depth, 2 pure-picture-plane and 4
  mixed (ASSUMED), each balanced for rotation type, same/different and angle, in random
  order (ASSUMED); block order random per subject (ASSUMED).
- One block per session, [[param:design.n_sessions]] = 8 sessions (ASSUMED within the
  paper's eight to ten). The plan is seeded by the subject id, so `--sub s01 --ses 3` is
  always the third block of s01's plan; run sessions 1 to 8.
- `configs/task-rehearsal.yaml` (dashboard: "Rehearsal, 20 trials") is a 20-trial
  stratified sample for checking a rig or the pipeline; it is not a session of the
  experiment.

## Analysis

Original (p. 703): "the reaction-time data presented below include only the 96.8 percent
correct responses". Fig. 2 plots the mean RT of correct "same" responses against angular
difference, separately for picture-plane (A) and depth (B) pairs; both were "strikingly
linear", from about 1 s at 0 deg to 4–6 s at 180 deg depending on the subject, with a slope
corresponding to roughly 60 deg per second. "Polynomial regression lines were computed
separately for each subject under each type of rotation. In all 16 cases the functions
were found to have a highly significant linear component (P < .001) when tested against
deviations from linearity. No significant quadratic or higher-order effects were found
(P > .05, in all cases)." Errors: 3.2% on average (0.6–5.7% by subject). Correct
"different" pairs: mean 3.8 s. Pure blocks: RTs "no more than 20 percent lower" than mixed.

`notebooks/analysis.ipynb` computes each of these from one or more runs' `*_trials.csv`:
the Fig. 2 panels with linear fits and the implied rate (deg/s); per subject and rotation
type, the linear component against deviations from linearity, F(1, k − 2), and the
quadratic and higher-order components against the within-angle error (orthogonal
polynomial contrasts over the angle levels); error rates; the mean RT of correct
"different" pairs; pure vs mixed means.

## Deviations from the original

Intended changes. Each row: what the paper did, what this version does, and why.

| Item | Original | Here | Reason |
|---|---|---|---|
| Apparatus | Line drawings reproduced by photo-offset on cards, seen through two circular apertures in a vertical black surface at about 60 cm | A computer display: the black surface and the two apertures are drawn, each aperture showing one image of the stimulus bank; sizes are set in degrees of visual angle from the rig file | Cards cannot be presented by software; the visual angles the paper gives (9 deg spacing, 4-5 cm drawings at about 60 cm) are kept |
| The ten objects | Ten specific shapes (Fig. 1) drawn by a Bell Laboratories program | Ten shapes generated under the paper's stated constraints (10 cubes, 3 right-angled elbows, two mirror-image sets of five with no self- or cross-congruence within a set), picked by a fixed seed | The original shape set is not given in the paper and its drawings are not redistributable; the constraints are |
| The drawings | Perspective projections plotted on a Stromberg-Carlson 4020 microfilm recorder | Perspective projections with hidden lines removed, rendered by the folder's own rasterizer (numpy) as anti-aliased 512-px images; picture-plane rotations are the image turned on screen (interpolated) | Reproducible and redistributable stimuli generated by a script in the folder |
| Response | Pull a right-hand lever for "same", a left-hand lever for "different" | Press J (right hand) for "same", F (left hand) for "different" on a keyboard | A keyboard is what a standard rig has; the hand assignment is the paper's |
| Warning signal | A warning tone, the pair half a second later | A small white dot between the apertures for the half second before the pair | alhazen v3.5.0 sounds tones only for trial feedback; it has no tone bound to an arbitrary task event yet, and faking one with the feedback beep would mislabel the event |
| Timing | An electric timer started with the pair and stopped by the lever | RT from the display flip that showed the pair to the frame on which the key was read (resolution one frame, about 17 ms at 60 Hz, plus keyboard latency) | Software timing on a frame-locked display |
| Between pairs | The black surface stayed in place while the cards were changed | After each answer alhazen blanks the window to its mid-grey background for the moment it takes to save the trial, then the black surface returns for the inter-trial interval | alhazen's trial engine ends every trial with a blank flip in the window colour; the black surface is part of each trial |

## Verification

Software verification only. This folder has recorded no participant data and claims no
replication of the original result.

- `tests/test_bank.py`: every object is 10 cubes in one chain with exactly 3 right-angled
  elbows; no shape has a rotational symmetry or is achiral; the five shapes of a set are
  pairwise non-congruent and not mirror images of each other; each shape is the mirror of
  one in the other set; each object's 7 views give two pairs at every angle 0–180 and hide
  no cube; the drawings' mean extent is the configured size and every drawing fits its
  aperture at any rotation; a mirror object seen from the mirrored azimuth is the drawing
  flipped left to right; the generator reproduces the committed bank (byte-identical on
  the machine that made it; pixel-equivalent required elsewhere); the binary-size ceiling.
- `tests/test_design.py`: 400 + 400 unique pairs, 20 depth and 20 picture-plane "same"
  pairs at each of the ten angles, depth pairs differ by the angle about the vertical
  axis, picture-plane pairs are one drawing turned by the angle, each "different" pair is
  its "same" twin with one object reflected, 1600 trials with each unique pair twice,
  8 blocks of 200 (2 + 2 pure, 4 mixed), one per session, plan fixed per subject.
- `tests/test_task.py`: trial logic on alhazen's fake clock with scripted inputs, for every
  outcome (correct and wrong for both pair kinds, timeout), unbound and early keys, the
  event order and the 0.5 s from warning to pair, RT from pair onset; params files load and
  agree with the bank; the session hook.
- `tests/test_simulate.py` and `tests/test_notebook.py`: `run.py --mode simulate --headless`
  on the rehearsal set end to end (real time), a whole 200-trial block of the main set in
  simulated time (`scripts/simulate_sessions.py`, alhazen's FakeClock), and the analysis
  notebook executed on the rehearsal's output. Run by hand on all 8 simulated sessions of
  one subject (1600 trials), the notebook recovered the planted pattern: linear RT
  functions of nearly equal slope for both rotation types (about 57 deg/s against the 60
  planted), no quadratic or higher-order trend, 3.8 s for "different" pairs. The
  simulated subject plants the paper's own numbers; recovering them shows the pipeline
  works, not that people behave this way.
